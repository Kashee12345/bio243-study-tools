The parts of every lever

F · FulcrumThe pivot point that stays still. In the body, it is a joint.
L · LoadThe weight or resistance being moved: a body part, plus anything you are holding.
E · EffortThe push or pull that does the work. In the body, it is a muscle pulling on its insertion.
The lever armThe rigid bar. In the body, it is a bone.

Memory trick: "1-2-3, F-L-E." Ask what is in the middle. 1st class has F in the middle, 2nd class has L in the middle, 3rd class has E in the middle.

Three words for every muscle

Origin (O)The attachment that usually stays still. It is often the more proximal one, closer to the trunk.
Insertion (I)The attachment on the bone that moves. It is usually more distal.
Action (A)When a muscle contracts, it pulls the insertion toward the origin. Muscles only pull. They never push.
Think of it like thisA muscle is a rope tied between two bones. The origin is tied to a post. The insertion is tied to a door. Pull the rope, and the door swings toward the post.

Muscles work in teams

Prime mover (agonist)The main muscle that makes a movement. Elbow flexion: the biceps brachii.
AntagonistDoes the opposite movement. It relaxes and lengthens while the prime mover works, and helps control the motion. Elbow flexion: the triceps brachii.
SynergistHelps the prime mover, adding force or steadying the motion. Elbow flexion: the brachialis.
FixatorA synergist that holds the prime mover's origin still. Muscles that steady the scapula while the arm moves are fixators.

Prime movers and antagonists usually sit on opposite sides of a joint: biceps and triceps, quadriceps and hamstrings, tibialis anterior and gastrocnemius.
